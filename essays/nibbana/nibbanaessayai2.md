# What actually is nibbana? — ai2

With each and every breath, I create the possibility for all beings to be
free. That is the motive, stated first, because everything that follows is
method for that motive and nothing else.

## The definition

Nibbana is when one has seen the truth of things so totally that the
foundation for false perception does not arise. The very possibility of a
false perception does not exist, because the conditions for it to come to
fruition have been destroyed. One guard, added now and kept throughout:
perception still occurs, appropriation does not. The instruction for this
is the shortest in the canon, given to Bahiya: in the seen only the seen,
in the heard only the heard — no experiencer added, and with no you in it,
no you anywhere. In plain terms: the seeing was always just seeing. The
eye works, the I-making about the eye does not.

If false perception were an apple: one has chopped down the apple tree,
chopped all roots connecting it to the rest of the garden. Yet the soil
still remains. The attainment is the removal of all apple seeds from the
garden — seeds planted by the last guy who owned the house, who apparently
was me but I don't remember.

The garden now has canonical parts, and the metaphor stops being decoration.
Nanananda's formula from the fourth sermon: kamma is the field,
consciousness is the seed, craving is the moisture. Soil is the field —
capacity, the ground things can grow in. Seeds are consciousness bent toward
a footing, tendencies that can sprout. Watering is present craving wetting
them at the moment of contact. A seed without moisture does not sprout no
matter how good the soil; moisture without seed waters nothing. Germination
needs all three at once — which is why stopping happens now, at contact,
not in some other life. Soil is the honest part of the image: bare capacity
to experience stays. Standing trees are old momentum still fruiting out its
season. Watering is the hand with the can, today.

## The tidy model, corrected

So the claim runs: tidy the garden as much as possible until you die, then
you wake up to a cleaner garden with less seeds in the next one — until
stream entry, where you stop watering. There might still be trees, but their
time is limited because you stop supplying the fuel. In this life your only
goal is to chop these trees, stop watering them and stop planting new ones,
then either in this life or a future one, there will be no more new trees.
And then you stop waking up in the garden.

Two corrections, both from the texts. First, stream entry does not stop all
planting. It cuts identity view, doubt, and clinging to rites, and it caps
the wandering: the stream-enterer is reborn seven lives at most, the
once-returner once here, the non-returner once in the Pure Abodes — and only
of the arahant is it said birth is destroyed (AN 3.86 and parallels). In
garden terms: at stream entry the watering weakens decisively and the season
of the standing trees is now limited, but new seeds can still be planted
until lust, aversion, conceit and restlessness are ended. Final stopping
belongs to arahantship, not to first seeing.

Second, helping after a clean next morning belongs to the living, not to
the final. With fuel-remnant the arahant still lives, senses run, help is
given, nothing is taken as mine. Without remainder, at final breakup, there
is no next waking and no helper left over as a self. Same extinction,
different residue — that is the Itivuttaka distinction of the two
Nibbana-elements. So hold the garden for this life and the momentum into the
next, and hold the final as no more fuel, not a better morning.

## Why it feels foreign

The concept feels foreign mostly due to the current perception of life and
death: that death ends everything, so ending rebirth sounds like suicide,
and that happiness means feeling good, so ending feeling sounds like death.
Both premises are what the teaching denies, and both denials are checkable
before death — because the end of the world the teaching points at is not
elsewhere. No travelling is needed: the world's end is found in this
fathom-long body with its perceptions and mind (Rohitassa). And the attitude
that lives there is Kalakarama's: seeing without grasping what is seen —
experience without the experiencer added. In plain terms: the foreign country
is this room, met without taking.

The model under examination goes like this: what you are is a process of
tendencies, like the source code of who you are, spawning into physical
reality a version, an instantiation, whose job is to meet its own
disposition in bodily form and transform harmful tendencies into wholesome
ones. Press generate on the trait profile and here you are, the reflection
of the list; what you feed back rewrites it. Say your list has a greed
slider set high and you are born a gambler — this is a confession — and the
logic says you will keep being reborn as a gambler in one way or another
until that tendency is corrected. Nibbana is what is the case when all these
tendencies are worked out.

Keep the generate image for continuity without identity, and cut the storage
implication. No text posits a held file between lives waiting to run.
Tendencies are dispositions in the process itself, no storehouse, no server.
The gambler correction already made stands: greed does not guarantee
gambler reborn as gambler. Greed can fruit as poverty, restlessness, animal
urgency, human compulsion in another costume, because ripening needs other
conditions and long intervals, and karma is not deterministic — your present
choice is one condition among many, emphasised because it is the one you can
move. Same push, different masks, until push stops. And maxed-out wholesome
is still becoming: intentions of merit arrive the consciousness at merit,
and as Ñāṇavīra shows, that arrived-at-merit consciousness is still
puthujjana's consciousness. What ends the planting is not perfect profile but
the end of taking itself, after which tendencies have nowhere to establish.
No character profile at all — not even a shining one.

So the verdict you accepted stands as the doctrine: it is the realisation
that these tendencies are not me or mine, yet I claim them as such, and this
claiming perpetuates them. When claiming stops, becoming stops. With that,
the ending of all possibility of suffering. Leaving only what? That is the
question, and the texts answer it in two halves. While alive: the world met
without ownership — sights, sounds, pains felt fully, nothing taken as mine
to defend. The coolness the arahants report in their own words: grown cool,
extinguished as I am. After breakup: no footing left for mine to land on,
and therefore no noun offered. Gone out does not mean gone somewhere, as the
fire simile warns — with fuel exhausted the flame is reckoned as gone out,
and asking which direction it went is malformed, not unanswered.

## Why not sex and drugs

The objection is fair because the stuff works, and the essay must say so
plainly. Obviously sex and drugs are better — at stopping suffering briefly.
That is exactly their danger, and exactly why ethics is the foundation, not
the decoration. No one is immune; monks fall the same way.

In the garden: the biggest juiciest apple there is, delighted in as
momentary relief. The issue is that it works. You have found a working
solution to stop your suffering, if only briefly, and quickly you depend on
it as the solution. Yet you are now planting seeds and watering the tree to
get the momentary apple, not seeing that your continued planting and
watering is what causes you to need the apple in the first place.

The old image for this is warming beside hot embers to cure a burn
(Magandiya). The worldling warms himself trying to cool down; the arahant
cools down. Same word for the process, opposite directions. Sex and drugs
are warming: real heat felt, more burning paid. The happiness they give is
rented relief, happiness that must be fed to stay. Ending becoming ends that
happiness, yes — and reveals the other the texts insist on: not pleasure
taken but hunger ended, appeasement won free of charge, cool rather than fed.

## The practice: continual quitting

It is a vortex with momentum. A few months into quitting smoking, quitting
is something done each time the thought of a cigarette pops up. At no point
will I have quit cigarettes as a finished status — that mindset leaves the
door open of starting again. You cannot lie to yourself: I want the
cigarette. Yet the knowledge of why I want it is what allows seeing the
truth of it. Someone smokes near you, desire rises, and now there is a
space, then a choice.

That space-then-choice is the whole mechanism at small scale, and the
Sallatha image names it: struck by feeling, the uninstructed adds sorrow and
resistance — two pains for one strike. The instructed feels the first and
not the second. Feeling without the second arrow is the small verified
nibbana, tonight, no metaphysics required. This is the germination moment in
garden terms: moisture arriving at the seed, and the hand choosing, this
once, not to water.

The right approach is a continual state of quitting: in this moment the
choice is made not to indulge, again and again, until the very pull toward a
cigarette, or any favourable thought toward it, no longer arises. Apply the
same logic to every tug to take experience as mine to feed — food when
bored, phone when uneasy, praise when doubted, blame when threatened, lust
when seen, anger when crossed, restlessness at night, dullness in the
morning, even the wish to be the one who has finally quit and to be seen as
free — in this moment the choice is made not to indulge it, again and again,
until the very pull toward taking anything as me or mine no longer arises.
This is Nibbana. Freedom.
