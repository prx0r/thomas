## Beat 7 — textual anchor: what the words mean

**S1. Samsara = wandering on.** `sam` (together/round) + `sarati` (moves/flows).
Standard gloss, uncontested. No citation needed beyond a Pali dictionary;
state it as etymology, not doctrine.

**S2. Beginninglessness (SN 15.1, Anamatagga Samyutta, repeated across SN 15.1-20).**
Pali: "Anamataggo'yam, bhikkhave, samsaro. Pubba koti na pannayati
avijjanivarananam sattanam tanhasamyojananam sandhavatam samsaratam."
Bodhi: "This samsara is without discoverable beginning. A first point is
not discerned of beings roaming and wandering on hindered by ignorance and
fettered by craving." Status: VERIFIED (stock formula, cross-checked).
Use: answers the ramble's line-121 question — the texts refuse a first
cause; wandering has no evident starting point.

**S3. Ignorance defined (MN 9 Sammaditthi; SN 12.2 Vibhanga; SN 35.80 Avijja Sutta).**
"Not knowing about suffering, not knowing about the origin of suffering,
not knowing about the cessation of suffering, not knowing about the way
leading to the cessation of suffering — this is called ignorance."
Status: VERIFIED (Ñanamoli/Bodhi MN 9; Bodhi SN 12.2 §15; Thanissaro SN 35.80).
Use: replaces the ramble's loose "not seeing reality" with the textual
fourfold not-knowing. Also note MN 9's mutual conditioning: with taints
arises ignorance, with ignorance arise taints — prefigures Nanavira §24.

## Beat 8 — the crux: self-sustaining not-seeing

**N1. Seeing = seeing conditionality (Nanavira, A Note on Paticcasamuppada §7).**
"He who sees the Dhamma sees paticcasamuppada; and he has also said that
the Dhamma is sanditthika and akalika, that it is immediately visible and
without involving time." Status: VERIFIED verbatim (nanavira.org).
Use: establishes the essay's test — Dhamma is checkable now, not believed later.

**N2. Being a self = taking self at face value (Nanavira §10).**
"The puthujjana takes what appears to be his 'self' at its face value; and
so long as this goes on he continues to be a 'self', at least in his own
eyes (and in those of others like him). This is bhava or 'being'... The
arahant has altogether got rid of asmimana... and does not even think 'I am'.
This is bhavanirodha, cessation of being... Quite clearly, the idea of
re-birth is totally irrelevant here." Status: VERIFIED verbatim.
Use: the essay's definition of the maze — being continues by being taken
for granted. Note the closing caveat for honesty: Nanavira brackets rebirth
as irrelevant to the structural seeing; the essay should flag this rather
than hide it (links to Analayo A3 below).

**N3. Ignorance conditions itself (Nanavira §§23-24).**
"Paticcasamuppada depends upon non-seeing of paticcasamuppada... Avijja
depends upon avijja... though it is impermanent and constructed, avijja is
so stable." Status: VERIFIED verbatim (substance; exact §23-24 lines on box
via earlier read). Use: the hard crux, lines 125-135 of the ramble. No first
point because the condition is self-maintaining structure.

**N4. Judge and accused (Nanavira §25).**
"However much a man may step back from himself to observe himself he cannot
help taking avijja with him. There is just as much avijja in the
self-observer as there is in the self-observed... In reflexion avijja is
the Judge as well as the Accused, and the verdict is always 'Not Guilty'...
That is why the Dhamma is patisotagami, 'going against the stream', it gives
the outside view inherently unobtainable by unaided reflexion."
Status: VERIFIED verbatim. Use: centerpiece quote for Beat 9; introduces the
graft/outsider teaching as method, not authority worship.

**N5. The three self-views reflexion cannot escape (Nanavira §25, via MN 2
Sabbasava).** "With self I perceive self; with self I perceive not-self;
with not-self I perceive self." Status: VERIFIED (quotes Sabbāsavasutta
M.i,8). Use: shows why stepping back never lands outside — optional cut if
tight.

## Beat 5/6 — confusion in perception; why we keep it

**NN1. Going round vs going out (Nanananda, Nibbana Sermon 3).**
"With the help of the simile of a whirlpool, we attempted an explanation of
the terms samsara on the one hand, and Nibbana on the other, that is to say
'going round', or samsarana, and 'going out', or nissarana."
Status: VERIFIED (local box text Nibbana-03.txt). Use: the essay's canonical
definition beat; whirlpool = maze with textual warrant.

**NN2. Abyss = painful bodily feeling (Nanananda, Sermon 3, via SN 36).**
"Monks, abyss is a synonym for painful bodily feelings... When the
uninstructed worldling is contacted by a painful bodily feeling, he sorrows,
grieves, and laments... This is called an uninstructed worldling who has
not risen up in the bottomless abyss." Status: VERIFIED (local box text +
Bodhi SN 36.4 parallel). Use: grounds maze/sandcastle in feeling tone —
why beings keep delighting in sense pleasures knowing no other way out.

**NN3. Thirst from feeling (Nanananda, Sermon 3).** "The thirst of craving
arises in beings in various forms of existence because of painful feeling."
Status: VERIFIED (local box text). Use: one-line mechanism for the sandcastle.

## Method honesty + open questions (Analayo — keeps the essay peer-reviewable)

**A1. Rebirth integral, not soul (Rebirth in Early Buddhism, Conclusion).**
"Rebirth is an integral component of the teachings of the historical Buddha
in the way these have been preserved in the earliest textual materials at
our disposal. The early Buddhist doctrine of rebirth does not involve a
simple mind-body duality, nor does it posit an unchanging entity to be
reborn." Status: VERIFIED (local box text). Use: one sentence placing the
essay — samsara includes continuity beyond one life in the sources, not as
add-on.

**A2. Reasonable belief, not proof (same, Conclusion + Ch. III Summary).**
The body of cases changes rebirth "from a religious creed to a reasonable
belief," still short of proof; neither affirming nor denying is the
legitimate starting stance, with the four truths as the workable alternative
definition of right view. Status: VERIFIED (local box text). Use: answers
"because Buddha said so" before it is asked; pairs with Nanavira's graft —
trust as working hypothesis, not submission.

**A3. Memory is reconstruction (same, Ch. III.2).** Remembering as
imaginative reconstruction under present attitude (Bartlett), Schacter's
sins, false memories felt as real as true ones; past-life regression
therapeutically useful, evidentially weak. Status: VERIFIED (local box text).
Use: guards the metal-detector analogy — a felt gold bar is not automatically
gold; verification criteria required.
