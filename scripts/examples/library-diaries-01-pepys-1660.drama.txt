[wry, brisk reader voice, diary-paced] January the first, 1660. I went to bed early, and woke with my head full of business. [soft pause] This is Samuel Pepys, twenty-six years old, a navy clerk in London, beginning the most famous diary in English on a winter morning. He does not know the plague is coming. He does not know London will burn. He knows his accounts need balancing.
[warmer] Listen to the day assemble itself. Up. To the office, where the clerks scratch and the Navy's money moves in columns. [soft pause] Pepys checks the accounts twice — he always checks twice — and finds them true, and the pleasure of a balanced book warms him till dinner. Dinner at home — a good leg of mutton, he will note that, he notes everything. Music in the evening; Pepys loves music the way other men love horses. A quarrel with his wife about money, made up by bedtime. [small smile] An ordinary day, four hundred years gone, preserved because one man wrote it down before sleep.
[slow, fading] And so to bed. [long pause] That is how he ends it. Every night. And so to bed.
