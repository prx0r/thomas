## A1. Rebirth integral, no soul-thing — Conclusion

"Rebirth is an integral component of the teachings of the historical Buddha
in the way these have been preserved in the earliest textual materials at
our disposal. The early Buddhist doctrine of rebirth does not involve a
simple mind-body duality, nor does it posit an unchanging entity to be
reborn. Instead, continuity during life and beyond is conceived of as a
changing process of a plurality of interrelated mental and physical
phenomena." Use for: placing the essay; continuity without continuant.

## A2. Reasonable belief, open stance — Conclusion + Ch. III Summary

The case material moves rebirth "from a religious creed to a reasonable
belief," still short of proof. Right view does not require accepting rebirth
on mere faith, but it does require an open attitude and forbids flat denial;
the four truths stand as the workable alternative definition a practitioner
can verify now. Use for: answering "because Buddha said so"; pairs with
Nanavira's graft — trust as working hypothesis.

## A3. Memory rebuilds — Ch. III.2

Remembering is imaginative reconstruction under present attitude (Bartlett);
Schacter's transience, absent-mindedness, blocking, misattribution,
suggestibility, bias; false memories feel as real as true ones; past-life
regression therapeutically useful, evidentially weak; verifiable detail is
the bar. Use for: guarding the metal-detector analogy — felt gold is not
automatically gold.
