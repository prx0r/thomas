# Shelf: secular challenge and the reply (Batchelor vs Anālayo)

For the essays' honesty: the modern reader's position, stated fairly, with
Analayo's reply beside it. Do not quote Batchelor beyond the theses below
without fetching; do not present Analayo's critiques from memory.

## B1. Batchelor's secular frame — After Buddhism (2015), Secular Buddhism
(Yale 2017), "A Secular Buddhism" (Journal of Global Buddhism 13, 2012)

Four distinctive points claimed as the Buddha's own, bracketing what he
shared with Indian religion: (1) conditionality / dependent arising as
process; (2) four noble tasks not truths — embrace suffering, let go of
grasping, experience stopping, cultivate the path as things to do, not
propositions to believe; (3) mindfulness of specificity; (4) self-reliance,
no surrender of autonomy to teachers. Rebirth and karma-as-rebirth treated
as ancient Indian soteriology (his "Buddhism 1.0"), dispensed with in a
post-metaphysical dharma ("Buddhism 2.0"). Status: POINTERS from public
summaries this conversation — fetch the 2012 paper / Yale volume before
quoting. Use for: steelmanning the modern reader.

## B2. Analayo's replies — critiques of Batchelor, Ingram, Burbea, secular
Buddhism broadly

Published critiques arguing the wholesale rejection of rebirth misreads the
early discourses and inherits colonial missionary polemics rather than
science; "neither affirming nor rejecting" is the legitimate starting
stance, flat denial is not. Status: ATTESTED (encyclopedia summary this
conversation) — fetch the actual papers before quoting. Use for: the reply,
beside B1, never instead of it.
